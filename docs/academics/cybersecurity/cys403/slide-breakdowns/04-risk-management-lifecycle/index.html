CYS403 — Chapter 4: Risk Management Lifecycle

1. Risk management

Risk management is the identification, assessment and prioritization of risks. A mature program contains two formal processes: risk identification/assessment and risk control.

2. Accountability

  • Cybersecurity: leadership in addressing risk.
  • IT: builds and maintains secure systems.
  • Management: allocates resources and prioritizes security concerns.
  • Users: contribute to early detection and proper response.

3. Lifecycle

Identify → measure → prioritize risks, then evaluate control measures using cost-benefit analysis, implement controls and measure their effectiveness. Risk management is cyclical, not a one-time activity.

4. Asset inventory, classification and valuation

Inventory assets first without prejudging value. Assets include people, procedures, data/information, software, hardware and networking. Classification groups assets by sensitivity/security needs; categories should be comprehensive and mutually exclusive. Valuation then assigns relative value based on factors such as cost, profitability, revenue and public image.

5. Ranking and TVA

After valuation, rank assets. Assess threats and vulnerabilities, prioritize threats for each asset and combine the results into a Threat–Vulnerability–Asset (TVA) worksheet. The central relationship is: threat exploits vulnerability → asset suffers loss.

Exam focus

  • Match each community of interest with its responsibility.
  • List asset categories and sample attributes.
  • Explain classification vs valuation vs ranking.
  • Interpret a TVA worksheet or threat/vulnerability scenario.
