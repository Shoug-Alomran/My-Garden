CYS403 — Chapter 2: Risk Management Fundamentals

1. Risk

Risk is the possibility of losing something of value. NIST SP 800-30 frames risk as a function of the likelihood that a threat source exercises a vulnerability and the resulting impact on organizational assets.

2. Core terminology

  • Asset: something valued by the organization.
  • Threat: potential danger.
  • Attack: actual danger/event.
  • Threat agent: something with potential to cause a threat.
  • Vulnerability: weakness that could be exploited.
  • Exposure: opportunity for a threat to cause loss.
  • Countermeasure/Safeguard: measure used to protect systems.
  • Residual risk: risk remaining after safeguards are applied.

3. Asset + threat + vulnerability

A threat and a vulnerability are not enough in isolation. Risk emerges when a valued asset can be harmed because a threat can exploit a vulnerability. Always identify the three parts in scenarios.

4. Qualitative vs quantitative

Risk analysis can be quantitative (numerical/monetary calculations) or qualitative (relative judgments such as low, medium and high). Both provide different capabilities and may be needed for a complete picture.

Exam focus

  • Differentiate threat vs attack and threat agent vs attacker.
  • Identify asset, threat and vulnerability from a scenario.
  • Define residual risk.
  • Explain risk using likelihood and impact.
  • Compare qualitative and quantitative analysis.
