SLIDES 2–8

Why risk matters to the business

The chapter begins with protecting information, systems and users, then connects organizational risk, IT risk, security investment, business-driven security, governance/control and policy.

Enterprises depend on automation and integration. Risk management therefore needs to cross IT silos and integrate with existing organizational risk-management practices. The executive–IT knowledge gap must be bridged so both sides can find middle ground in the digital landscape.

Mnemonic — BRIDGE
Business priority · Risk crosses silos · Integration · Digital landscape · Gap between executives and IT · Executive/IT middle ground.

Risk and value

Risk and value are two sides of the same coin. Risk is inherent to enterprises. Enterprises achieve return by taking risks, so eliminating every risk can also eliminate opportunities for value creation. The goal is effective management of risk, not zero risk.

Students typically make this mistake
Do not write: “Risk management eliminates all risk.” The slides explicitly warn that over-avoidance can remove opportunities for value creation.
SLIDES 9–11

Val IT vs Risk IT vs COBIT

FrameworkSlide meaning
Val ITGovernance framework used to create business value from IT investments.
Risk ITEnd-to-end, comprehensive view of risks related to IT use.
COBITIT governance model supporting value delivery and understanding/managing IT risk.

Risk IT complements and extends COBIT and Val IT to provide more complete IT-governance guidance.

Mnemonic — V R G
Val IT = Value · Risk IT = Risk · COBIT = Governance.
Common confusion
Risk IT is not the framework for creating value from IT investments—that is Val IT. COBIT is broader governance guidance.
SLIDES 12–18

Business-driven security and management

The CIA triad shown is Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability. Business-driven security means creating a security strategy aligned with business needs.

Organizational requirements depend on the organization's mission. Different organizations therefore have different security requirements, and security must make sense and be cost effective.

Mnemonic — MDC
Mission first · requirements Differ · security must be Cost-effective.

Complete security solution

Functional requirements define the security behavior of a control measure and are selected based on the risk-management process. Assurance requirements provide confidence that the security function performs as expected and are a critical part of the security program.

Students typically mix this up
Function = what the control should do. Assurance = confidence that it performs as expected.

The management view in the slides emphasizes People + Process + Technology and explicitly includes Governance + Risk Management.

SLIDES 19–22

Governance vs management

Cybersecurity governance is presented as an integral part of overall corporate governance.

GovernanceManagement
Evaluate → Direct → MonitorPlan → Build → Run → Monitor
Oversight and direction from business needs.Execution and operation.
Mnemonic — EDM
Evaluate · Direct · Monitor = Governance. Think “executives determine direction.”

Governance should integrate with overall risk analysis/management and ensure IT infrastructure meets requirements, supports company strategies/objectives and includes service-level agreements when outsourced.

Security blueprints

Blueprints provide structure for organizing requirements and solutions, ensure security is considered holistically, and support identification/design of security requirements.

Exam cue
If the scenario asks for a structure that organizes security requirements and solutions as a whole, the slide concept is security blueprint.
SLIDES 23–34

Policy hierarchy and implementation

The overarching organizational policy (management's security statement) is shaped by its environment: regulations, laws, organizational goals, organizational objectives and shareholders' interests.

Hierarchy: Overarching organizational policy → Functional implementing policies → Standards, Baselines, Procedures and Guidelines.

High-level policies are interpreted into functional policies. Functional policies are derived from the overarching policy and form the foundation for implementation. Policies gain credibility through top-management buy-in.

Functional policy examples

Data classification; certification and accreditation; access control; outsourcing; remote access; acceptable mail and Internet usage; privacy; dissemination control; sharing control.

Mnemonic — DCAORAPDS
Data · Certification · Access · Outsourcing · Remote · Acceptable use · Privacy · Dissemination · Sharing.

Standards, procedures, baselines and guidelines turn management objectives and functional policies into actions for employees.

Security baselines

Baselines are benchmarks that provide a minimum level of security configuration, establish consistent implementation, and are platform-specific. Slide examples include VPN setup, IDS configuration and password rules.

Mnemonic — SPBG
Standards · Procedures · Baselines · Guidelines = implementation layer below functional policy.

POP QUIZ

Final check

1. Why should an enterprise not try to eliminate every risk?

Because risk and value are linked; eliminating all risk can also remove opportunities for value creation.

2. Which framework focuses on business value from IT investments?

Val IT.

3. Which framework provides an end-to-end view of IT-related risks?

Risk IT.

4. What three verbs summarize governance?

Evaluate, Direct, Monitor.

5. What is the difference between functional and assurance requirements?

Functional requirements define control behavior; assurance requirements provide confidence that the function performs as expected.

6. What are the three elements of the CIA triad?

Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.

7. What does a security blueprint provide?

A structure for organizing requirements and solutions and considering security holistically.

8. What gives policies credibility?

Top-management buy-in.

9. Name the four implementation document types grouped below functional policy.

Standards, procedures, baselines and guidelines.

10. What is a security baseline?

A minimum, consistent, platform-specific security configuration benchmark.
