CYS403 — Chapter 1: Security Risk Management, Governance & Control

Slide-by-slide study breakdown focused on the concepts most likely to require explanation or application.

1. Why cyber risk is a business issue

Modern enterprises depend on automation and integration, so cyber risk cannot stay inside an IT silo. Management and IT must connect technical risk to business requirements, resources, processes and organizational information. The goal is not to eliminate every risk: risk and value are linked, and excessive risk avoidance can also eliminate opportunities.

2. IT risk, value and governance

  • Val IT: governance framework for creating business value from IT investments.
  • Risk IT: end-to-end view of risks related to IT.
  • COBIT: IT governance model that supports value delivery while understanding and managing IT risk.

Risk IT complements Val IT and COBIT to create broader governance guidance.

3. Business-driven security

Security decisions should be justified by business needs and risk, not technology for its own sake. A complete security solution combines security functions/controls with assurance requirements that provide confidence the security function performs as expected.

4. Governance, control and policy

Governance aligns security with organizational objectives and decision-making. Controls implement protection, while information-security policies establish expected behavior and direction. These concepts connect business priorities to operational security.

Exam focus

  • Explain why cyber risk is a business priority.
  • Differentiate Val IT, Risk IT and COBIT.
  • Explain why eliminating all risk is not the objective.
  • Connect business requirements → IT resources/processes → organizational information.
  • Distinguish a security function/control from assurance that it works as expected.
