People shape project outcomes

Stakeholder Engagement

A stakeholder can affect, be affected by, or perceive being affected by a project. Management is not “sending updates”; it is continuously understanding interests, influence, expectations, and the relationships needed to create value.

IdentifyAnalyzePrioritizeEngageMonitor
01 · Discovery

Identify early, revisit often

Discover
Sponsor, users, team, regulators, suppliers, operations, affected groups
Describe
Role, interest, expectations, influence, attitude, impact
Record
Stakeholder register with accountable owner
Update
At phase changes and when context shifts
Stakeholder register: identification information, assessment information, and classification. It is a living project document and may contain sensitive judgments, so access should be deliberate.
02 · Prioritization

Power–interest grid

High power · High interest

Manage closely. Involve in key decisions, resolve concerns quickly, and build a durable working relationship.

High power · Low interest

Keep satisfied. Give concise, relevant assurance; avoid flooding them with detail.

Low power · High interest

Keep informed. Invite feedback and use them as informed advocates or domain experts.

Low power · Low interest

Monitor. Use lightweight communication and reassess if power or interest changes.

Other models

Power/influence, impact/influence, and the stakeholder cube add dimensions. A salience model considers power, legitimacy, and urgency.

Directions

Upward to sponsor/governance; downward to team; outward to customers/vendors/public; sideways to peers and other managers.

Beware labels

Classification guides effort, not respect. A low-power user may hold critical knowledge or gain influence later.

03 · Strategy

Move from current to desired

An engagement assessment matrix compares today’s stance with the stance needed for success.

Unaware

Does not know the project or its likely impact. First create awareness in relevant language.

Resistant

Aware and opposed. Listen for legitimate loss, risk, incentives, or trust problems before persuading.

Neutral

Aware but neither supportive nor resistant. Clarify relevance and invite useful participation.

Supportive

Understands and supports change. Maintain trust and give meaningful roles.

Leading

Actively champions success. Equip them with evidence, authority, and feedback channels.

Key principle: engagement is tailored. Do not aim to make everyone “leading”; seek the level needed for the project and stakeholder.
04 · Interaction

Communication that changes understanding

Plan the message

Who needs what information, why, when, in what format/channel, from whom, and how feedback will be captured?

Choose the channel

Interactive for ambiguity; push for known recipients; pull for large information repositories.

Listen actively

Ask, paraphrase, confirm, surface emotion, and distinguish positions (“what”) from interests (“why”).

Handle conflict

Collaborate/problem-solve for durable agreement; compromise when time matters; avoid or accommodate only when context justifies it.

Monitor relationships through observation, feedback, issue logs, sentiment, participation, decision latency, and adoption signals. A delivered message is not proof of shared understanding.

05 · Responsibility

Ethical and practical care

Transparency

Explain impacts, limits, assumptions, and decision criteria honestly.

Inclusion

Seek voices that are easy to miss, especially those affected without formal authority.

Confidentiality

Protect sensitive stakeholder assessments; separate observations from unsupported judgments.

Accountability

Assign owners and dates to commitments; close the feedback loop.

06 · Retrieval practice

Check your judgment

A regulator has high power but little day-to-day interest. How should the team respond?
Keep the regulator satisfied with concise compliance evidence and required consultations; reassess interest when decisions or regulations change.
What does a stakeholder engagement matrix compare?
Each key stakeholder’s current engagement level with the desired level needed for project success.
A user resists a rollout. What is the best first move?
Listen and diagnose the underlying interests, losses, risks, or misunderstandings before choosing an engagement response.
Why must stakeholder identification repeat?
Stakeholders, influence, interest, project impacts, and relationships change throughout the lifecycle.
