Q1
What is the precise definition of "Ethics"?
✅Correct Answer: b)
Ethics is the study of morality through a rational examination of moral beliefs and behavior — it is a field of inquiry, not the legal system (a), not a purely religious directive (c), and not just a private, consequence-free code (d).
Q2
What type of ethical relativism holds the view that an action is morally right simply if an individual approves of it?
✅Correct Answer: b) Subjective relativism
Subjective Relativism holds that each individual decides right and wrong for themselves — an act is right if that person approves of it. Cultural Relativism instead ties morality to one's culture, not the individual alone.
Q3
If an employee reports a bad or illegal practice strictly to someone inside their own organization without public exposure, what does the text prefer to call this?
✅Correct Answer: b) Internal reporting
Reporting a bad practice strictly within the organization, with no public exposure, is internal reporting. External whistleblowing specifically refers to exposing the practice outside the organization (e.g., to the media, regulators, or the public).
Q4
What is the fundamental difference between Act Utilitarianism and Rule Utilitarianism?
✅Correct Answer: c)
Act Utilitarianism looks only at the consequences of the specific act in question. Rule Utilitarianism instead asks whether the act follows a general rule that, if universally adopted, would maximize happiness overall. Neither uses the categorical imperative (that's Kant) or perfect duties (also Kantian language).
Q5
Why is Subjective Relativism explicitly classified as a "non-workable" ethical theory?
✅Correct Answer: b)
Subjective Relativism is "non-workable" because letting each person decide right and wrong for themselves gives it no real persuasive force in resolving moral disagreements — there's no shared standard to appeal to. It has nothing to do with perfect duties (a Kantian concept) or group-level reasoning.
Q6
In Virtue Ethics, how does Aristotle define the virtue of courage in relation to its corresponding vices?
✅Correct Answer: b)
Aristotle's Doctrine of the Mean places courage between two vices: cowardice (an excess of fear) and rashness (a deficiency of fear, i.e. recklessness). Option (c) describes the mean for a different virtue (friendliness), and option (d) mixes in Kantian duty language that doesn't belong to Virtue Ethics.
Q7
What typically happens when an organization actively punishes or discourages internal reporting of bad practices?
✅Correct Answer: a)
When internal reporting is punished or discouraged, bad practices tend to escalate rather than resolve — until someone feels forced to go outside the organization and blow the whistle externally. Nothing about punishing internal reporting builds trust (b) or triggers automatic legal resolution (c).
Q8
What major critique is leveled as the "Case Against Social Contract Theory" regarding individuals who are fundamentally unable to uphold their side of the contract?
✅Correct Answer: c)
Social Contract Theory is criticized because it can unjustly treat, or leave without clear guidance, people who cannot fully uphold their side of the contract (e.g. drug addicts, criminals, or the mentally ill) — since the theory's protections are framed around mutual, reciprocal obligation. Option (b) is actually a Kantian idea (treating people as ends, not means), not this specific critique.
Q9
Under the Society-Professional relationship, what must a professional society do if it is granted legal licensing by society?
✅Correct Answer: d)
In exchange for the legal licensing granted by society, a professional society takes on obligations to serve the public interest, avoid harming society, maintain professional standards, and exercise due care grounded in its specialized knowledge. It is not simply an agent for client commands (a), nor does it prioritize profit over stakeholders (c).
Q10
In a professional setting, a software engineer signs a contract containing a non-disclosure agreement and a non-compete agreement. What professional duty is being structurally managed here?
✅Correct Answer: d)
Non-disclosure and non-compete agreements structurally manage an employee's obligation of loyalty — protecting an employer's trade secrets and specialized field knowledge both during and after the employment relationship ends. This is unrelated to Kantian "perfect duties" (a) or professional licensing (b).
